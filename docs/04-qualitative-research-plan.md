# Qualitative Research Plan: People, Politics and Who Really Drafts

_Status: v0 · 2026-11-21 · To be run only with ethics clearance, ideally with an academic partner_

## Why qualitative
Quantitative data shows **what** is fragmented. It cannot show **why** reforms are designed the way they are, why people comply in form but not in substance, or how politics and capability shape outcomes. These are the mechanisms that decide whether any redesign works.

## Framing rule
Study **systems, incentives and capability**, never named individuals. "Incompetent people" and "toxic politics" become **testable system properties**:
- capability → *how roles are designed, staffed, developed and held to performance*;
- politics → *how decisions are actually made versus how they are formally made*.

This framing is more defensible, safer for the author, and more useful: systems can be redesigned, while individuals rotate.

## Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **Drafting power:** reform proposals and governing-body decisions are drafted by HQ secretariats; member states mostly amend at the margin. Reforms are therefore designed by those being reformed | Draft decision texts (secretariat) vs adopted texts show small changes; consultation records show few field inputs | Large member-state rewrites; structured field consultation in design |
| Q2 | **Field-feedback gap:** designers have limited field operations exposure, and no mechanism brings field evidence into design | Low HQ↔field mobility; no user research or pilots with measured results before scale-up | Systematic field testing, rotation, field co-design |
| Q3 | **Performance management cannot act on performance** (capability problem is systemic) | Near-uniform positive ratings; few performance separations; JIU/OIOS findings on performance management | Differentiated ratings, consequences, development pathways |
| Q4 | **Politicised senior appointments** weaken managerial capability at the top | Appointment practices driven by nationality balancing over competence; JIU findings on executive-head selection | Merit-based, transparent selection with published criteria |
| Q5 | **Risk asymmetry:** staff and managers rationally avoid collaboration because blame is entity-specific and credit is shared | Interviews and audit findings citing reliance on other agencies as a risk | Audit reliance accepted in practice |
| Q6 | **Toxic or siloed culture** reduces information flow and speaks-up behaviour, so problems surface late | Published staff surveys and independent culture reviews in several entities | Healthy survey results where data exists |

_Public evidence to collect first (to verify, not cite from memory): JIU reports on performance management, executive-head selection and organisational culture; published entity staff-engagement and workplace-culture surveys; independent reviews of workplace culture commissioned by entities; OIOS evaluations._

## Methods

| Method | Tests | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft-vs-adopted text analysis** (quantitative text diff) | Q1 | Executive boards publish compilations of draft decisions and the final adopted decisions (e.g. UNDP/UNFPA/UNOPS, UNICEF, UN Women, WFP). Measure % of text changed, by topic. Fully public, low risk, novel |
| **Process tracing** of 3 reforms (2018 UNDS repositioning, BOS/CBO, UN80 WP14) | Q1, Q2 | Who drafted, who was consulted, what field evidence was used, what changed after consultation |
| **Semi-structured interviews** (30–40) | Q1–Q6 | OMT chairs, BOS/CBO focal points, RCO operations staff, HQ policy staff, board delegates (Fifth Committee, executive boards), donor-capital officials. Anonymous; with consent |
| **Anonymous survey** of country operations staff | Coordination tax, Q5, Q6 | Hours per year on BOS/CBO/UN INFO reporting; realised vs projected savings; top obstacles; decision times |
| **Positive-deviance cases** (3–5 countries) | Counter-evidence | Countries where CBO, common premises or shared services worked: what was different? |
| **HQ vs field staffing analysis** | Q2 | CEB HR duty-station data (public), aggregate only |

## Sampling
10–15 countries chosen for variety: large vs small UN presence, humanitarian vs development, CBO status (from UN INFO), regions. Include Türkiye as an author-known case, but not only Türkiye.

## Ethics and protection (author is UN staff)
1. **Partner with a university or think tank** so the research runs under formal research-ethics approval (consent, anonymity, data protection). This protects interviewees and the author.
2. Obtain **ethics office clearance** before data collection.
3. **No internal or confidential documents**; interviewees speak in a personal capacity and anonymously.
4. Report only aggregated findings; no quotes that could identify a person, office or small country team.

## Outputs
- Evidence on the *mechanisms* behind fragmentation, feeding back into `02-target-design-v0.md`
- Design implications: e.g. field co-design and pilots before scale-up, independent reform-design teams, performance-management redesign, audit reliance
