# Qualitative Evidence Plan: People, Politics and Who Really Drafts

_Status: v1 · 2026-11-21_
_Decision (author): **no new primary research.** Use existing published research based on surveys and interviews (secondary qualitative synthesis)._

## Why qualitative evidence
Quantitative data shows **what** is fragmented. Qualitative evidence explains **why** reforms are designed the way they are, why compliance is formal rather than real, and how capability and politics shape outcomes.

## Framing rule
Treat capability and politics as **system properties** (role design, performance management, appointment practices, incentives, how decisions are really made), never as judgements about named individuals.

## Hypotheses to test against existing evidence

| # | Hypothesis | What would support it | What would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **Drafting power:** HQ secretariats draft reforms and governing-body decisions; member states amend at the margin | Studies or evaluations describing secretariat-led design; small member-state changes | Evidence of member-state-led design |
| Q2 | **Field-feedback gap:** reform design lacks field evidence and piloting | Evaluations finding top-down design, weak country consultation | Documented field co-design and measured pilots |
| Q3 | **Performance management cannot act on performance** | JIU/OIOS findings; staff survey results | Evidence of differentiated performance management |
| Q4 | **Politicised senior appointments** | JIU findings on executive-head and senior selection | Merit-based, transparent practices |
| Q5 | **Risk asymmetry** discourages inter-agency reliance | Survey or interview findings citing audit/accountability risk | Reliance working in practice |
| Q6 | **Culture** (silos, fear of speaking up, harassment) delays problem detection | Published culture surveys and independent reviews | Healthy results where data exists |
| Q7 | **Coordination tax:** BOS, CBO, OMT and UN INFO reporting cost more time than they return | Survey data on time spent on coordination/reporting vs realised savings | Evidence of net savings |

## Method: structured evidence synthesis
1. Search and collect sources (2016–2026 priority; earlier for reform post-mortem).
2. Record each source in `data/evidence/` with: citation, method (survey n / interviews n), date, entities covered, quality rating.
3. Code findings against Q1–Q7 (supports / refutes / mixed), with a short extract and page reference.
4. Rate the strength of each hypothesis: number and quality of sources, consistency, recency.
5. Flag gaps where no published evidence exists. Those gaps become recommendations (e.g. "the system does not measure its coordination costs").

## Limitations to state openly
- Published evidence can be dated, entity-specific, or shaped by those who commissioned it.
- Self-reported survey data (e.g. BOS savings) is weaker than measured data.
- If Q7 (the coordination tax) has no direct published measurement, we state it as a gap rather than an established finding.
