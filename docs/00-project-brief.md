# Project Brief — UN System Operating Model Analysis

_Status: working draft · Last updated: 2026-11-21_

## 1. Central question

**Which few changes (leverage points) would make the UN system deliver value for input comparable to best-in-class global organisations — and who exactly has the authority to make each change?**

Sub-questions:
1. Where does the UN system lose efficiency and effectiveness, and how much does it cost? (the "fragmentation tax")
2. Why does fragmentation persist even though the member states behind the entities are largely the same?
3. Why did earlier reforms (Delivering as One 2006, UNDS repositioning 2018, management reform 2019, UN80 2025) under-deliver?
4. Which interventions sit at the highest leverage, and which body/person can enact each?

- **Public sources only.** No internal or restricted documents. Every claim cites a public source (document symbol, dataset, URL + access date).
- **Ethics clearance before any external sharing or publication** (Staff Regulation 1.2; entity information-sensitivity policy). Keep a record of the clearance.
- **System-design framing, not blame.** Name *positions and bodies*, not individuals. Findings are about incentives and rules, not people.
- **Confidence levels** on every finding (High / Medium / Low) based on evidence quality.

## 4. Analytical framework

### 4.1 Benchmark split
| Domain | Benchmark | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Back office (procurement, finance, HR transactions, ICT, premises, travel, fleet) | Best-in-class private Global Business Services (P&G, Unilever-type federated multinationals), and best UN internal cases | Unit cost, cycle time, quality |
| Mission delivery | Peer multilaterals / humanitarian orgs | Cost per result, speed, reach |

### 4.2 Leverage points (Meadows)
Classify every proposed intervention by leverage level: parameters (budgets, headcount) → information flows → rules & incentives → goals, decision rights, paradigm. Working hypothesis: most past reforms acted at low leverage.

Candidate high-leverage interventions (hypotheses to test):
1. **Funding architecture** — earmarked voluntary funding as root driver of competition and duplicated systems.
2. **Shared-services-by-default** ("comply or explain").
3. **Single-audit / mutual reliance** accepted by oversight bodies.
4. **Transparent unit costs** across entities and country offices.
5. **Mandate sunset clauses.**
6. **System-wide KPIs in leaders' compacts.**

### 4.3 Tracer processes (business models)
Each tracer gets: context diagram → functional flow → cross-functional swimlane → flowchart of key decision points, with measured cycle times/costs where available.

| # | Tracer | Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Donor dollar → beneficiary | Intermediation layers, overheads, reporting burden |
| T2 | Common procurement across entities (LTA lifecycle) | Mutual recognition in theory vs practice |
| T3 | Hiring one person | Time-to-hire vs benchmarks |
| T4 | GA mandate → implementation → reporting | Mandate accretion, reporting load |

### 4.4 Positive deviance
Find where the UN already performs at world-class level (e.g. global shared service centres, large-scale supply/logistics) and ask why those cases work — scale what exists rather than import only from outside.

### 4.5 Reform post-mortem
For each major past reform: what was recommended, what was implemented (JIU acceptance/implementation tracking, SG progress reports), why the gap.

## 5. Flagship products

### 5.1 Unit-cost transparency (entity and country-office level)
Target metrics: cost per procurement action, per payment, per hire, per m² of premises, ops staff per $ delivered, support-cost ratio.

**Data reality (to be confirmed):** spend data is public at entity and often country level (CEB financial statistics, IATI, UN INFO, UNGM procurement statistics); **transaction counts are rarely public.** Plan:
- Tier A — directly computable from public data (e.g. support-cost ratios, procurement volume per entity per country, BOS cost avoidance per country).
- Tier B — estimated with stated assumptions.
- Tier C — not computable → itself a finding ("the system does not publish its own unit costs") and a concrete recommendation.

Every figure carries its tier and confidence.

### 5.2 Decision-rights map ("who can really change this")
For every recommendation, a transparent map of:
- **Instrument** needed (GA resolution, executive board decision, specialized-agency assembly decision, SG bulletin / administrative instruction, HLCM/CEB decision, donor funding agreement, entity head directive).
- **Who decides / approves / can block / influences** — bodies and positions (e.g. Fifth Committee, ACABQ, executive boards and their member states by regional group, SG, Deputy SG, USGs/ASGs such as DMSPC, DOS, DCO, entity Executive Directors/Administrators, CEB/HLCM, Board of Auditors, OIOS, JIU, major donors).
- **Voting/decision rule and calendar** (session dates, consensus norms).
- **Membership** of each deciding body (public data), so readers see which member states sit where.

Format: RACI-style matrix + network graph, generated from the same structured data.

### 5.3 Collaborative evidence wiki (later)
Structured data (entities, bodies, capabilities, processes, bottlenecks, causes, sources) → rendered as mind map, diagrams and pages. Edits are suggestions that go through review; every node requires a source.
