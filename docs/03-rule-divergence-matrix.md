# Rule Divergence Matrix (v0)

_Purpose: show, rule by rule, how many different versions of the same operational rule exist across UN entities, who sets each one, and what that divergence costs. This is the evidence behind "no aligned policies"._
_Status: Row 1 complete from a primary source; rows 2–8 scoped, sources listed. 2026-11-21._

## Method
For each rule: (1) number of distinct versions across entities, (2) the body that sets it (decision-rights link), (3) whether the variation is justified by mandate or only by history and negotiation, (4) observable cost of divergence.

## Row 1: Indirect cost recovery (programme support cost rates) ✅

Source: **JIU/REP/2025/4**, *Review of the policies and practices for determining the rates of programme support costs in organizations of the UN system* (`data/raw/jiu/JIU_REP_2025_4.pdf`), paras. 15–22, 37–40, 49–55.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Nominal rates** | At least **8 distinct standard rates**: 13% (UN Secretariat + 10 orgs), 11% (UNRWA), 7–10% (ICAO), 7–9% (UNESCO), 8% (UNDP, UNFPA, UNICEF, UN Women), 7% (FAO, IAEA, ITU, UN Tourism), 6.5% (UNHCR, WFP), ~5.1% equivalent fee (UNOPS), 5–13% (IMO) |
| **Actual (weighted) rates 2019–2023** | Range **3.6% to 13.5%** |
| **Cost model** | Full cost recovery in 14 orgs, incremental in 6, mixed in 1. Even the basic model differs |
| **What the rate covers** | Differs by entity (e.g. some include security, resource mobilisation, ethics; others cover 2–3 functions only) (JIU table 2) |
| **Basis for the rate** | "In many organizations **not based on actual costs**"; the process is "to a great extent **market driven** and highly dependent on **negotiations with donors**" (para. 37) |
| **Main donor pushback** | Comparison with lower rates of other UN entities (11 orgs); donor legal limits (8 orgs) |
| **Who sets it** | Each entity's legislative or governing body (UNDP/UNFPA/UNICEF/UN Women jointly through their executive boards; WFP Executive Board; WHO Assembly/EB; etc.) |
| **History** | The 13% benchmark dates from UNDP Governing Council decision 80/44 and GA res. 35/217 (**1980**) |
| **JIU's 2025 ask** | Governing bodies should *consider* adopting a stand-alone policy **by end 2028**; publish policies **by end 2027**; define direct vs indirect costs **by end 2028**. JIU accepts "complete alignment may not be possible" |
| **UN80 link** | WP14 Action 67 "Harmonize most indirect and programme support costs": no milestone on the dashboard as of Nov 2026 |

**Interpretation:** 45 years after the first benchmark, the system still lacks a common **definition** of indirect cost, let alone a common rate. Rates are set by donor negotiation, not by cost. So: (a) earmarking is under-priced (the core subsidises it, consistent with P6 in `02-target-design-v0.md`); (b) entities undercut each other; (c) shared services cannot be priced consistently across entities. **Divergence is driven by history and negotiation, not mandate.** Confidence: High.

## Row 2: Procurement and mutual recognition (partial)

Source: HLCM "Mutual Recognition Examples" (May 2025) (`data/raw/ceb-hlcm/`); HLCM Procurement Network summaries.

| Dimension | Finding so far |
|---|---|
| Mutual Recognition Statement | Signed by executive heads of ~23 entities (HLCM PN 33rd session, 2023) |
| Practice | A UNICEF-led global freight tender serves 12 entities, but **"these entities implement separate contracts"**. Recognition removes re-tendering, not duplicated contracting |
| Friction | "Mutual recognition allows an entity to rely on another entity's procedures without further evaluation. However, during the implementation, **offices are required to provide additional documents**" |
| Türkiye | FAO's sub-regional office in Türkiye set up local LTAs (travel, events, printing, translation) that many entities piggy-backed on via the OMT, cited as a good practice |
| Still needed | Formal solicitation thresholds, contract-committee review thresholds and local delegation of authority per entity (from procurement manuals: UN PM, UNDP POPP, UNICEF, UNFPA, UNHCR, WFP, WHO, FAO, IOM, ILO) |
