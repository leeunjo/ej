Sources, method and disclaimer

Disclaimer

This is a personal analysis by the author, based solely on publicly available sources. It does not represent the views of the United Nations or any of its entities. Positions and bodies are named; individuals are not.

Sources used so far

  • UN System Chief Executives Board (CEB), financial statistics: revenue by contribution type, revenue by government contributor, expenses by country (2021–2025). Accessed November 2026.
  • CEB human resources statistics: staff by duty station (2025). Accessed November 2026.
  • Joint Inspection Unit, JIU/REP/2025/4, review of policies and practices for determining programme support cost rates.
  • CEB High-Level Committee on Management, Mutual Recognition Examples (May 2025); Procurement Network session summaries.
  • UN System Chart entity list (public dataset), used for entity groups.

Method

  • Four zoom levels of one system: context (L0), functions (L1), actors (L2), decision logic (L3), with a purpose layer (L-1) above.
  • Layers on the same diagrams: work, money, decisions, accountability, information.
  • Bottlenecks are hypotheses, each with evidence, a root-cause loop, decision rights and options.
  • Qualitative evidence comes only from published research based on surveys and interviews.
  • Figures are reproducible from the raw data and scripts in the project repository.

Confidence levels

High: official data or reports, directly computed or quoted. Medium: inference from official data. Low: single source or observation. Not measured: no public data exists.

Known limits

  • A "presence" means an entity reporting spending in a country, not necessarily an office.
  • Published evaluations may be dated, entity-specific, or commissioned by the bodies they assess.
  • Process diagrams are illustrative until validated against each entity's public rules.
